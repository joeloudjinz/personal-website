The shop belongs to several people. Some put money in, some put work in, some both. Hanotna's capital ledger is their shared diary: what each partner put in, what the shop is worth, what a trading cycle earned, and how the profit is split. It is the feature the app exists for, and the one with the most rules.

### A ledger with no edits

Every injection, withdrawal and expense is a row in one ledger, in one of fifteen categories. Nothing is edited. A mistake is corrected by a reverse entry that points at the row it undoes, so every balance can be rebuilt from the log at any time. Partners can put money in as dinars, or in EUR, USD and stablecoins at a rate they type.

### Ownership follows the money

A partner's capital account is what they have really put in, and their share of the shop is that account over the total. Nobody owns a percentage by decree. A working partner can also hold a work share: a fixed cut of each cycle's profit, taken before the investors are paid. The work shares together can never pass one hundred per cent, a rule the database enforces as well as the app.

### Trading cycles

Profit is measured over a cycle. Closing one takes a photograph of the business, works out the cycle's income, splits it, and opens the next cycle in the same transaction, so there is never a moment without one.

### Principal only, never interest

A partner can lend the shop money. The loan is tracked apart from ownership and repaid at face value. The app never models interest (riba) in any form, and the code has no field where a rate could even be written.

<ForEngineers>
  The headline profit is a flow calculation, revenue less cost of goods less expenses, with cost of goods taken from a snapshot at the moment of each sale. The growth-in-value calculation shown above is kept as the reconciliation, and an optional physical count turns it into an actual figure. Closing is one Firestore transaction that re-reads the cycle, writes the end snapshot and the split, opens the next cycle with that snapshot as its start, and flips a metadata flag that lets sales proceed. The cap of one hundred per cent on work shares is enforced twice: an optimistic-concurrency aggregate in the app, and a rule on the database that reads the post-write state.
</ForEngineers>
