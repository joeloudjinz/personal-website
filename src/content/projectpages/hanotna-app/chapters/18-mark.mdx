The mark is a small storefront: a scalloped canopy, a sign band, two posts, three figures and one caramel star rising from a notch in the roof. It was drawn as flat artwork with the help of an image model, then rebuilt as seven vector parts so the app could assemble it on screen and so every icon size would stay crisp.

### Seven parts, one timeline

The splash screen stacks the seven parts and builds the shop front-to-back over 1,920 milliseconds on one easing curve: canopy, sign band, posts, the three figures, and the star last, turning in as it grows. The figure above plays the same timeline in the browser, and stands still for anyone who has asked their system for less motion.

### From a picture to vectors

Hand-drawing the mark was tried four times and never matched the approved artwork. So the artwork was traced instead, one ink at a time.

### Why true negative space

The shapes are separated by real gaps rather than by colour, so the mark sits on any background and the star still reads when the colour is taken away, which is what themed icons on Android and tinted icons on iOS require.

<ForEngineers>
  Each pixel is assigned to its nearest reference colour, with the references refined once from the mean. Each ink is traced with potrace at its own speckle threshold, which took the star from 46 spurious paths to one. The traced paths are reassembled with the brand hexes, since the scan read them a few values off. The light, dark and mono variants are derived by substituting fills, so the geometry cannot drift between them, and every variant is verified with resvg at three sizes, in mono, and with a fill-stripped diff. The parts share one viewBox and one fit transform, so they compose pixel for pixel.
</ForEngineers>
