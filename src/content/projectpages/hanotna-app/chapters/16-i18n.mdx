Every string a user sees goes through one catalogue, and the catalogue is kept honest by tools rather than by memory. Arabic arrived late, after months of English-only screens, which is exactly when a pipeline earns its keep.

### A glossary before a single batch

Forty-eight terms were settled first, so that a cycle, a draw, a write-off and a profit share would be the same word on every screen. Then the English was cut into thirteen batches along screen boundaries, each carrying for every string its key, its context, its role on the screen, its placeholders and a length limit where the role has one.

### A checker that says no

Every returned batch is checked by a tool before it is accepted. The key set must match exactly and placeholders must survive. No Eastern-Arabic digits may appear, and the currency symbol must never be followed by a digit. Length limits hold, every plural carries all six Arabic forms, and the tokens that must not be translated are checked against a list. A failure writes a re-ask file rather than a patch.

### Kept at parity

1,980 keys in English and 1,980 in Arabic, none missing and none extra, 485 of them with placeholders and 78 with plurals, across nineteen areas of the app. The parity is measured, not assumed. A guard checks the Arabic file's contract on every test run: its digits, its arrows, where the currency sits, and all six plural forms. A string written after those batches comes in English and Arabic in the same change, under the same key rules.

<ForEngineers>
  Keys follow an area, screen, role and slug pattern, are assigned once and never renamed, and anything used in two places takes the common prefix. The template file keeps its keys sorted with each metadata block beside its key, a hygiene rule a tool enforces so parallel slices merge cleanly. Stored values stay English on the wire and are translated only at display, with a display twin for each unit, category and status. Guards reject a translated string persisted into data, and a raw wire value inside a translated sentence.
