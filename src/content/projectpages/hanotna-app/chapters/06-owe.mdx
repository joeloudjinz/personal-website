A shop owes money too: to the supplier who delivered this week, to the landlord every month, to the people behind the counter. Hanotna records all three in the same ledger the partners read, so the money the shop owes is never a surprise at the end of a cycle.

### Bills that keep their own balance

One bill per delivery. The goods total is what the shop owes, payments are added against it, and the balance is worked out rather than stored, so it cannot drift. Transport on a delivery is posted once as an expense and the bill remembers that it was, so it cannot be posted twice.

### Rent covered until a date

The owner sets the rent up once, with the landlord and the date the shop is paid until. Each payment says how many months it covers, and the paid-until date is derived from the payments, never typed again. Gaps and overlaps are shown, not blocked.

### Wages as a proposal, not a decree

Each employee has a pay profile: a rate by the month, the day or the hour, the days of the week they work, and a start date that anchors their pay period. Days off are recorded as exceptions, paid or unpaid. When a period ends the app proposes a figure, the owner can change it before paying, and both the computed and the paid amounts are kept.

Advances, settlements and rent payments all write ordinary expense rows into the capital ledger, and the feature that wrote a row is the only one allowed to void it. That way there are never two ways to undo the same money.
