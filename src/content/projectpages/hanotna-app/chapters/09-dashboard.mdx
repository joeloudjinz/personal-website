The home screen is built around one idea: the counter first, then what wants a decision, then the money. A cashier standing at the till sees the four tools they reach for. An owner sees the same, then everything that needs attention, then the day's figures.

### The counter tools

Four tiles: new sale, find a product, scan a barcode, and customers. New sale is the only caramel tile on the screen, because it is the one thing the shop does most.

### Needs attention

A triaged list, most urgent first. Customers past their due date. Money owed to the shop and money owed to suppliers. Staff pay due. Rent due, with the date it was due. Drafts left open. Each row opens the list it came from. When the list is empty the screen says so, in words rather than in colour.

<Quote text="Overdue debts, open bills and drafts land here." source="The empty state of the attention list." />

### The money, for those who may see it

Today's sales stand as the one large figure on the screen, with the week and the month beneath, and the gross profit beside them when the person is allowed to see cost. Below that, suppliers and open bills for the partners, a forecast of what the stock would fetch at list price, and a short feed of what happened last.

### Severity you can read

Four tints carry four meanings across the app, each always paired with a word and a mark: a past date or a loss, money outside the till, work in progress or a forecast, and all clear. Nothing on the home screen is signalled by colour alone.
