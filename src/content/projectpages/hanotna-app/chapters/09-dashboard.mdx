The home screen is built around one idea: the counter first, then what wants a decision, then the money. Staff, partners and the owner all see the four tools, what needs attention and today's sales. Partners see more beneath: the week and the month, the profit, the drawer, the suppliers and a forecast.

### The counter tools

Four tiles: new sale, find a product, scan a barcode, and customers. New sale is the only caramel tile on the screen, because it is the one thing the shop does most.

### Needs attention

A triaged list, most urgent first. Customers past their due date. Money owed to the shop and money owed to suppliers. Staff pay due. Rent due, with the date it was due. Drafts left open. Money owed to suppliers is listed for partners, and staff pay due for the owner. Each row opens the list it came from. When the list is empty the screen says so, in words rather than in colour.

<Quote text="Overdue debts, open bills and drafts land here." source="The empty state of the attention list." />

### The money, for those who may see it

Today's sales stand as the one large figure on the screen, for the whole shop, because the drawer is counted against them. Partners also see the week and the month, and the gross profit with its margin. Then comes the drawer, with what it should hold and when it was last counted, then suppliers and open bills, then a forecast of the profit sitting in the stock. Everyone sees a short feed of what happened last.

### Severity you can read

Four tints carry four meanings, each always paired with a word and a mark: a past date or a loss, money outside the till, work in progress or a forecast, and all clear. Nothing on the home screen is signalled by colour alone.

Profit week by week, whether the month's costs are covered, and the whole year are in [Insights](#insights), under More.

  Which figures a person sees is decided by the screens. The database rules protect the partners' capital, and they do not hide cost or turnover from a signed-in member.
