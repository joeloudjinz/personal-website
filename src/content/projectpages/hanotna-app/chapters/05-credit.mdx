Many customers of a neighbourhood shop pay some now and the rest later. Hanotna treats that as the normal case. A sale records what was received, the unpaid remainder goes onto the customer's balance with an optional due date, and the shop can see at a glance who owes what and who is overdue. Credit needs a named customer.

### Oldest debt first

A repayment is spread across the customer's open orders, oldest first by the date of the sale, and the app shows where the money will land before it is committed. A payment can also be put against one order only. The rule is how shop credit is settled by hand, written down once as a pure function that a test can check.

### Not revenue until it is paid

Every amount the shop actually receives is a row in a payments log, and revenue is the sum of that log. An unpaid remainder writes no row, so a credit sale is not revenue until the customer pays. The log is never edited: cancelling a sale writes a negative row, and the history of what was collected stays visible.

### Forgiving a debt is a row too

A debt can be written off, with a note saying why. The write-off is itself a log row that is kept out of revenue, so the books still add up, and it can be reversed later if the customer comes back. Staff, partners and the owner can each record a payment or write a debt off. No interest is ever charged on anything owed.

<ForEngineers>
  Each change to a customer's balance writes an event with the amount, the reason, who and when. The database lets nobody edit or delete those events, and a balance cannot fall without one in the same write. Undoing a write-off is kept for the admin role.
</ForEngineers>
