A sale at the counter is one screen, New sale, and one tap. The cashier finds or scans the products. The amount received follows the total: it is typed only to leave part on credit, in the Customer & credit sheet, and credit needs a named customer.

Confirm sale creates the order and confirms it in the same step, so a confirmed sale leaves no draft behind, and the stock, the money and the customer's balance all move at once. When part goes on credit the button names the amount, and when it cannot be used the reason stands above it. A sale is not queued for later: without a connection, Confirm sale and Save as draft wait. Staff, partners and the owner can all sell, edit an order and take a return.

### What one tap does

Every line in the cart freezes the product's name, SKU, unit, list price and cost price at the moment it is added. An order is a record of what was sold, not a view onto the catalogue, so a price change next week never rewrites last week's order. A per-line price override exists for the real world, and its sheet watches for a stray zero: at a hundred times the list price or more it asks the cashier to check, and does not block.

Discounts come in two kinds, a flat amount or a percentage, and a percentage is worked out again on what the customer keeps whenever the order is edited or goods come back. Delivery is one cost, added at the sale or later on the order's page. The total is the subtotal less the discount plus delivery less anything returned, and it never goes below zero. Every line total and every discount is rounded to the nearest dinar, a half up, and saved that way, so the screen shows what the books hold.

### Never sell what is not there

A product sale that would push stock below zero is refused outright. There is no override. The rule used to warn and let the cashier force it.

### Drafts

A draft takes no payment and carries no delivery, and Save as draft says what it left out. Nothing leaves stock until it is confirmed, so a draft may hold more than the shelf does.

### Editing a confirmed order

Edit order changes the lines, the discount and the note. It does not change the customer, the amount paid or the due date. When a line, a price or the discount has changed, Review changes shows what the save will do before it is made: per product, what more leaves stock or what goes back, then the total before and after, what is already paid, and whether the customer's credit goes up or comes down. A change to the note alone is saved without it. If the customer has paid more than the new total, the sheet says what to hand back in cash, and a refund row is written. A walk-in sale has no customer to owe a rise, so the rise is collected on the spot.

### Returns, line by line

A return is composed first and committed once. The cashier picks quantities per line and marks each Back to stock or Write off, for damaged goods that come back for the money only. A confirmation lists each line, where it goes and the money before and after. Several lines return in one atomic transaction, and one bad line rejects the whole basket. The order stays confirmed, its total is recomputed, and if the customer has now paid more than the new total a refund row is written to the payments log. A return that would raise the total is refused.

### Two phones, one order

Confirming a draft, saving or deleting one, Edit order, Return items and a change of delivery are each taken from the copy of the order on screen. If the order has changed on another phone since, the action is refused, nothing is written, and the screen brings the order up to date and says "This order changed on another phone. It is now up to date. Try again." A cancel is refused only when the order's status has changed. Recording a payment and writing off a debt make no such check.

<Callout tone="info" label="Why cancelling keeps the history">
  Cancelling an order writes a negative row to the payments log instead of erasing the positive one. The order still shows what had been collected, and the log still adds up.
</Callout>
