A sale at the counter is one screen and one tap. The cashier picks products, types what the customer hands over, and confirms. Hanotna creates the order and confirms it in the same step, so a quick sale never leaves a draft behind, and the stock, the money and the customer's balance all move at once.

### What one tap does

Every line in the cart freezes the product's name, SKU, unit, list price and cost price at the moment it is added. An order is a record of what was sold, not a view onto the catalogue, so a price change next week never rewrites last week's receipt. A per-line price override exists for the real world, and the sheet that takes it watches for typos: an override far from the list price asks before it is accepted.

Discounts come in two kinds, a flat amount or a percentage, and a percentage is re-applied to the new subtotal whenever the order is edited. Shipping is one cost on a confirmed order. The total is the subtotal less the discount plus shipping less anything returned, and it never goes below zero.

### Never sell what is not there

A product sale that would push stock below zero is refused outright. There is no override. The rule was reversed from an earlier "warn and let them force it" after it became clear that selling goods that do not exist corrupts the stock count, and a stock count the owner cannot trust is worse than a sale that has to wait.

### Returns, line by line

A return is composed first and committed once. The cashier picks quantities per line and says whether each goes back on the shelf. Damaged goods come back for the money only. Several lines return in one atomic transaction, and one bad line rejects the whole basket. The order stays confirmed, its total is recomputed, and if the customer has now paid more than the new total a refund row is written to the ledger.

<Callout tone="info" label="Why cancelling keeps the history">
  Cancelling an order writes a negative row to the payments ledger instead of erasing the positive one. The order still shows what had been collected, and the ledger still adds up.
</Callout>

<ForEngineers>
  The order and its stock moves commit in one Firestore transaction. Stock is re-read inside the transaction, every affected product gets an adjustment row of type `order_confirm`, and the write set is bounded by the chapter's rule of two documents per product plus the order, at most one payment and at most one customer. An oversell throws `InsufficientStockException` from the adapter, which the quick-sale, order-detail and order-edit screens all handle. Seven call sites write stock, all through one shared primitive that chains moves across lines so two lines of the same product deduct twice.
